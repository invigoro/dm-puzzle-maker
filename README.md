# Daedalus

Puzzles for tabletop games. A game master makes a puzzle, sends the players a link, and they solve
it in their browser. The link never holds the answer, so nobody can peek. It's named after Daedalus,
who built the Labyrinth.

**Live site:** https://daedalus.invigoro.me/

## Using it

The home page lists the kinds of puzzle. Each opens a page for making one, and every one ends the
same way:

- **What the players read:** a title, an introduction (a riddle, an inscription, a hint at the
  order), and a line for when they solve it.
- **Send it:** Copy player link, or Preview as player. The player link holds the puzzle as it starts,
  and fingerprints to check the answer against, never the answer itself. The page says how long the
  link is, and warns when it's too long for one Discord message.
  - **Copy as a Discord link** copies it as `[title](link)`, which Discord shows as the puzzle's
    title alone.
  - **Shorten with is.gd** makes a short link, such as `https://is.gd/Ab3x9`, and copies it. It
    sends the player link to [is.gd](https://is.gd/), a free link shortener, which keeps it for good.
    That's the only time the site sends anything anywhere, and only when you ask. A short link opens
    the puzzle as it was when shortened, so make another after a change. Once there is one, the
    Discord link uses it.
- **The page's own address** holds the puzzle, answer and all, so a bookmark brings it back. It's
  compressed, so it never spells the answer out, but don't send it to your players.
- **Your puzzles:** every puzzle you make is saved in your browser as you make it, and listed on the
  home page, newest first, to open again or remove. **Export to a file** keeps a copy, and **Import
  from a file** brings puzzles into another browser, keeping the newer of any you have in both.

On the players' page, progress is kept in their browser, so a reload or a week between sessions
doesn't lose the board.

### Turning discs

Touching discs of stone, rimmed in bronze, each with six word-halves or pictures cut into its
spokes. The players turn the discs and swap halves at the bronze studs where they meet, until the
names are spelled out in order, from where the arrow points:
- **Seven discs:** six names round the centre disc.
- **Three discs**, as in Baldur's Gate 3's Defiled Temple: three names, where the three discs meet.
  A quick puzzle.

- **Where the names are read:** the two spokes that meet there have a channel sunk along them,
  edged in gold, and their stud has a gold ring.
- **Lettering:** each half reads down its spoke from its own disc's hub, every letter standing on
  the rim. So the two halves at a contact stand foot to foot, and a name counts either way round.
- **Moves:** tap a disc to turn it clockwise, Shift-click it to turn it back, or drag it round either
  way. Tap the ⇄ where two discs meet to swap the halves there. From the keyboard, Tab picks a disc
  or a contact, the arrow keys turn and Enter swaps. **Undo** and **Start over** are below the board.
- **Solving:** a solved board lights up the names, and shows the line for solving it. Or, if you
  choose, the board is checked only when the players press **Check the answer**, so they can't just
  turn until it's right. A wrong check says a line of yours (the trap it sets off, say), and the
  page counts wrong checks, kept in their browser. A board can't be checked twice without a move
  between.
- **Making one:**
  - **The discs:** three or seven, each drawn small. The names and filler follow the choice, and
    any beyond its count are kept aside in case you change back.
  - **The names:** six or three of them, each in two halves, in order clockwise from the arrow.
    **Any start** counts them in order from any of their contacts, and takes the arrow away.
    **Glow** lights each name as it comes right, for an easier puzzle; it's off unless you turn it
    on, as a player could then test names one at a time. **Start from the example** fills in
    made-up towns, to see how it works.
  - **Pictures:** a half can be words, a picture, or both. 🖼 beside any half opens a search of
    about 4,000 fantasy icons from [game-icons.net](https://game-icons.net/) and 2,000 more
    everyday ones from [Font Awesome](https://fontawesome.com/), together or one set at a time, and
    a puzzle can mix them. A picture stands at the rim end of its spoke like the letters, and the
    page names whoever made each picture on show, as their licenses ask.
  - **Filler:** the other 30 spokes. Blank boxes fill themselves with halves of place names (ASH,
    THORN, WICK…), and 🎲 picks others. Names that look right but aren't make the best filler.
  - **The start:** every piece anywhere, or 20, 10 or 5 moves from solved for an easier puzzle, with
    🎲 for a new scramble. Any scramble can be solved, and none starts with a name already in place.
    Or **set it by hand**: turn and swap a board of its own until it's as the players should find
    it, starting from the scramble, from solved, or from a fresh scramble. The page says how many
    names start in their places.
  - **Checking:** after every move, so the board solves itself, or only when the players ask, with
    a line for a wrong check. Names never glow when they check by asking, as that would tell them
    the answer a name at a time.
  - **Try it:** the board as the players will see it, to play, or solved.

### Word guess

A word or phrase to guess in a set number of tries: a password at a door, a name, or a riddle's
answer. Only its letters count, in capitals, with accents taken off.

- **How they try:** they type any letters, knowing only how many there are (and where the words
  break, unless that's hidden), or they arrange the answer's own letters as shuffled tiles.
- **What a try tells them:** letter by letter (blue for the right place, gold for in the answer
  elsewhere, counting repeated letters as the familiar game does), or only right or wrong. Marking
  letter by letter puts a quick fingerprint of each letter in the player link, so a programmer could
  read the answer from it; the maker says so beside the choice.
- **Tries:** one, three, six, ten, or as many as they like, with a line for running out. The tries
  made are kept in the browser, so a reload gives no more.
- **Hints:** as many letters as you allow, a letter at a time, each word's first letter first.
- **The line for solving it** is sealed with the answer, so the link holds it but only the right
  answer opens it.

### Maze

A maze to find the way out of, seen from above.

- **Making one:** small (8 by 8), medium (14) or large (20), or a size of your own from 4 to 30.
  Passages long and winding, or many short dead ends, with no loops (one way through), a few or
  many. 🎲 makes a new one.
- **Drawing it:** click a wall to put it up or take it down, or set the way in, or add up to four
  ways out, each with its own line: one leads on, another to the minotaur. Or put a picture in a
  cell. The maker shows the
  shortest way through to each, and says if one can't be reached.
- **Light:** lit, so the whole maze shows; by lantern, so the players see the cells near them and
  the passages they've walked stay drawn, like a map; or dark, so they see only the cells near them,
  and nothing stays drawn. By lantern or in the dark, you choose how near: up to 10 steps along the
  passages, never through a wall (2 in a straight passage is 2 cells ahead and 2 behind), or none,
  so they see only the walls beside them. Whatever the light, the link holds the whole maze, so a
  programmer could draw it.
- **Their view:** the whole maze, or a window 3 to 11 cells across round the party, which slides as
  they move. Past the edges looks like the dark, so by lantern or in the dark only counting steps,
  and landmarks, tell them where they are.
- **Pictures:** up to 20 in cells, from the icon sets, each with a line said on reaching it:
  landmarks to steer by, hazards to run into, and hidden hazards that show only once someone runs
  into them. A hazard's line is said and the party carries on, or goes back to the way in. The link
  holds every picture, so a programmer could find the hidden ones.
- **Playing:** move with the arrow keys (or WASD), by tapping the next cell, or by swiping. Steps
  are counted, and where the party is and has been is kept in the browser.

### Trapped floor

A room's floor of 5-foot squares, some trapped, to cross by minesweeper's rules: each safe square,
stood on, shows how many of the eight round it are trapped, diagonals and all, as on a D&D grid.

- **The room:** 3 to 20 squares each way. The party starts on a side or a square, and is going to
  another side or square.
- **The traps:** how many, placed at random (🎲 for others) or by hand on the plan. The start and
  the squares beside it are kept clear, so the first step tells the party something. **Fair**
  floors can always be crossed by reasoning from the counts, never by luck: the maker keeps placing
  traps until a careful solver gets across, and says when none turns up.
- **Kinds of trap:** up to six, each with a picture and what it does ("Darts from the walls: 1d4
  piercing, DC 12 Dexterity save for half"). Each trap is one of them at random.
- **When a trap springs:** its line is said and the party carries on, or they go back to the start
  (remembering where it was), or after so many traps they fail, with a line for that.
- **How they move:** spreading out onto any square beside one they've stood on, or one at a time as
  a token. Squares can be chalk-marked as suspected traps. Progress is kept in the browser.
- **Secrecy:** the link holds every square masked, never plainly, but the players' page has to open
  any square stepped on, so a programmer could open them all. For a table all in one room, **Play
  it full size** runs it on your own screen, and no link holds the floor at all.

### Pedestals

Pedestals, each with a riddle, and objects about the room to choose among: the players set the right
one on each. "Place the greatest weapon", and among a sword, a war axe and an olive branch, the
olive branch is right.

- **The pedestals:** up to 8, each with its riddle and the one object that's right for it. Riddles
  and objects can be words, a picture, or both.
- **Other objects:** up to 12 more, right for no pedestal, that only look as if they might be. All
  the objects lie scattered on the floor (🎲 for another scatter), so the right ones don't come
  first.
- **Checking:**
  - **When all are right:** the pedestals open the moment each holds its object, with nothing said
    before, so trying costs nothing.
  - **As each is placed:** a right object stays; a wrong one says your line (the trap it sets off)
    and drops back to the floor.
  - **When they ask:** once every pedestal holds something, the players press Check the answer. A
    wrong check says your line, and as much as you allow: only that it's wrong, how many are right,
    or which (those pedestals glow). The same objects can't be checked twice.
  - Wrong placings or checks are counted, and if you like, after so many the players fail, with a
    line for that.
- **Playing:** tap an object, then a pedestal, or drag it there. Tap a pedestal to take its object
  back. What's on each pedestal, and the count, are kept in the browser.
- **Secrecy:** the link lists the objects with nothing to say which are right or where they go.
  Judging pedestals one at a time puts a quick fingerprint of each in the link, so a programmer
  could find them.

### Cipher

A message in cipher, and a wheel to read it with: the alphabet runs round the outside, fixed, and
the inner ring turns, with the cipher's symbols. The first symbol on the inner ring has a mark, and
the letter the mark sits under is the wheel's setting.

- **The message:** up to 300 characters. Its letters are put in cipher; spaces and punctuation
  stay, or the letters run on in groups of five for a harder puzzle.
- **The symbols:** letters, runes, or a picture of your choosing for each letter (🎲 fills the
  rest). The ring can be mixed by a word (DRAGON gives D R A G O N B C E…).
- **The key:**
  - **One setting:** the whole message read with the mark under one letter. Turning finds it in a
    minute or so, so it's for an easy puzzle.
  - **Turning as it goes:** the mark moves a letter on after every letter, or every word. Noticing
    that is the puzzle.
  - **Autokey:** the mark goes under each letter of a starting word, then under each letter just
    read. Hide the starting word in the game.
  - **A keyword:** the mark goes under each letter of the keyword in turn, round and round, as
    Vigenère's cipher has. Hide the keyword in the game.
- **Checking:** when every box is filled with the right letters; by an answer the message gives (a
  password), with your line for a wrong one, a count, and failing after so many if you like; or not
  at all.
- **Read it for them:** if you like, the page shows what the wheel reads as it turns, or reads the
  message with the keyword the players type.
- **Playing:** drag the inner ring round, use the arrow keys, or the buttons. Write under each
  symbol the letter it stands for; at one setting, a letter goes under every symbol like it. The
  wheel, the letters and the count are kept in the browser.
- **Secrecy:** the link holds the message only in cipher, and the ring as the players see it, never
  the key or the answer. Anyone who reads the link has the same puzzle as the page gives them.

It pairs with [Sator](https://sator.invigoro.me/), which makes anagrams for puzzles,
[Stele](https://stele.invigoro.me/), which carves or inks text onto a weathered object, and
[Jabberwock](https://jabberwock.invigoro.me/), which writes text in made-up languages.

## Development

Requires Node.js 22.12 or newer. The repo pins 24 in [`.nvmrc`](.nvmrc).

```sh
npm install
npm run dev       # dev server with hot reload
npm test          # unit and page tests
npm run build     # type-check, then build to dist/
npm run preview   # serve the production build locally
```

The pictures are written to `public/icons/` before every dev server and build, from the
`@iconify-json/game-icons` and `@iconify-json/fa7-solid` packages. [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) says
where they come from, how each is credited, and how to update them.

Each kind of puzzle is a folder under [`src/puzzles/`](src/puzzles/) and an entry in
[`registry.ts`](src/puzzles/registry.ts). [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md#kinds-of-puzzle) says what
each side provides.

Every push to `main` runs the tests, builds the site and deploys it to GitHub Pages
([`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`](.github/workflows/deploy.yml)). Pushes to other branches run the
tests and the build without deploying ([`.github/workflows/test.yml`](.github/workflows/test.yml)).
